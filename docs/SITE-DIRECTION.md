# Site direction — from watch toy to personal website

A design plan, not an implementation. Nothing in `index.html`, `src/styles.css`,
`src/main.js` or `tools/**` has been touched by this document.

This is the second draft. The first one ended with eight open questions; you
answered all eight, and they are folded in below as settled decisions rather
than as things still to argue about. Where a recommendation changed because of
your answer, it says so.

Everything is grounded in the tree at `2b53952` and measured in a real browser at
1440×900, 900×760 and 390×800. Where a number appears, it is one I took.

---

## 0. The governing goal

Your words:

> **"The goal is for this project to not look like AI slop."**

Everything below is arbitrated by that sentence. It is not a mood; it is a test
that most of the decisions in this document can actually be run against. So it
needs unpacking properly, because "slop" is doing a lot of work and it means
something quite specific here.

### What it actually means for this site

**Slop is the look of something assembled quickly from defaults and never
inhabited.** Not ugly — usually the opposite. It is competent, generic, and
empty. The three symptoms, in the order a visitor notices them:

1. Placeholder content presented as real.
2. Several design vocabularies that don't know about each other.
3. Features that exist because they were easy, not because anyone wanted them.

The site has all three right now, in different amounts. Here is where it stands.

### 0.1 The single biggest live tell: every panel says *Lorem ipsum*

This is not one of several problems. It is the problem, and it is worth being
blunt about the size of it. Counted in the current tree:

| Panel | Fake items |
| --- | --- |
| About | 2 paragraphs, plus a dashed circle that says "Your photo" |
| Featured | 3 projects, invented, with invented dates |
| Currently | 4 rotating entries in `main.js` |
| Experience | 5 timeline entries — roles, years, descriptions, all invented |
| Book Reviews | 4 books with invented titles and invented authors |

**Nineteen fabricated items.** They are not lorem in the harmless "the designer
hasn't got the copy yet" sense either. `Lorem Ipsum Fellow`, `Amet Research
Assistant`, `Consectetur Principia` by `Physicus Ignotus` — the *shape* of a CV
is there and the substance is decoration. A visitor reads two lines and knows
they are looking at a demo, and once they know that, the watch stops being an
interface and becomes a screensaver. Everything measured to two decimal places
in `DESIGN_BIBLE.md` is spent by the third sentence of the About panel.

Two consequences that should change the plan's shape:

- **Real content is the highest-leverage work remaining, and it is the only work
  here that cannot be delegated, generated, or faked.** A convincing-sounding
  book review of a book you didn't read is *more* slop than lorem, not less —
  lorem is at least honest about being absent. This is the one place where the
  right answer is "you write it."
- **It should therefore move to the front of the phasing.** The previous draft
  had it in Phase 4, behind navigation, routing and affordances. That ordering
  optimises for shippable engineering increments, and it is wrong for this
  project: a nav bar that indexes five panels of lorem is a nicer demo, not a
  website. Real content is now Phase 1. See §8.

You do not need all of it at once. **Four honest sections beat seven padded
ones.** If you have not read four books worth reviewing, ship three. If Featured
has one real project you can write two paragraphs about, that is a portfolio;
three invented ones are not.

### 0.2 The project's real strength, and how to not spend it

The thing that makes this project not slop already is that **it is measured
rather than approximated.** That is unusual and it is worth naming precisely,
because the instinct when adding features is to approximate, and every
approximation spends a little of it:

- Sprite geometry derived by alpha-scanning the actual art, not eyeballed —
  `tools/build-dial-art.py` prints the numbers the CSS then uses.
- The power reserve's end stops at **40.7°** (AUF) and **138.9°** (AB), measured
  off the scale sprite's own pixels, so the hand covers exactly the 98.2° of arc
  that is drawn.
- The caseback rig's part positions given to three decimal places, generated by
  `tools/generate-rig-markup.mjs` and never hand-edited, with identity-at-rest
  machine-verified against the original render (full-stack diff 0).
- The demonstration sweep's easing chosen because `1-(1-t)²` has a linearly
  falling derivative, which *is* a flywheel coasting under constant friction —
  and every total is a whole number of turns so the hands land home with zero
  velocity instead of snapping.
- The interaction index's five marks lit by one `userSpaceOnUse` gradient across
  the whole face, so all five share one light source rather than each being lit
  in its own little box.

That last one is the pattern to internalise. Someone reached for
`objectBoundingBox`, noticed it would give five independent light sources, and
changed it. **Nobody would ever see the difference consciously and it is exactly
why the thing doesn't read as generated.** The comments in `styles.css` and
`main.js` are full of that kind of decision.

**The rule this gives us: nothing added from here may be approximated where the
existing work is measured.** If a new element needs a position on the case, it
comes from the art or from geometry, not from nudging until it looks right. If a
new motion needs a duration, it is derived from the thing it depicts. That is
slower, and it is the whole reason the site is worth building.

### 0.3 Motion must explain function or depth

This is the project's own rule (`DESIGN_BIBLE.md`, restated in `HANDOFF.md`):
*"Motion must explain assembly, mechanical function, panel navigation, or depth.
Do not add ambient floating, random parallax, neon glow, continuous camera drift,
or ornamental loops."*

It has already paid for itself once: the previous draft argued for retiring the
five infinitely-pulsing `.hint-dot`s, and they are gone — replaced by the scribed
`.hint-index`, which draws itself once after assembly and then stops. That is the
rule working.

Two proposals in this document are on the wrong side of it and are dropped in
this draft: the **idle re-cue on a timer** (a loop with a long period is still a
loop) and any **ambient tilt**. Pointer-driven tilt survives, conditionally — see
§7.

### 0.4 One vocabulary, not several

Conflicting idioms are what make a thing feel assembled rather than designed. The
first draft wrote seven careful rules about this — and applied them only to the
navigation chrome, which does not exist yet. Meanwhile:

**The panels — where a visitor spends every second of their reading time — are
generic dark-mode components.** Measured from the current CSS:

```css
.card { border-radius: 6px;
        border: 1px solid rgba(217,162,107,.3);
        background: linear-gradient(165deg, #111726, #0b101c);
        box-shadow: 0 40px 90px rgba(0,0,0,.65); }
.tag  { border: 1px solid rgba(217,162,107,.35); border-radius: 3px; padding: 7px 12px; }
```

A rounded rectangle with a hairline border, a subtle gradient fill, a big soft
drop shadow, and a row of bordered pill tags. There is nothing wrong with any of
it and it is on the theme colour — and it shares **no vocabulary at all** with the
watch. There is no 6px radius anywhere on the case. There are no boxed pills on
the dial. The specular light that governs every surface of the watch does not
touch the card.

Open About at 900×760 and the effect is stark: the watch is reduced to a blurred
gold sliver in the corner and what fills the screen is a card that could be from
any dark portfolio template, containing lorem, next to a dashed placeholder
circle. **That single screen is the slop risk, more than the navigation ever was.**

So the vocabulary rules in §6 apply to the panels first and the chrome second.
This is the largest change of emphasis in this draft.

### 0.5 Restraint beats feature-piling — deleting counts as progress

Three deletions are already in flight or recommended, and each one is a real
improvement rather than a chore:

- **The review-request form** — removed, and already done (commit `52f5b14`).
  Its markup (`#reqLayer`), CSS, four state fields and five handlers went with
  it, plus `.card-title-sm`, which it was the only user of. It had already lost
  its trigger; an earlier draft spent half a page finding it a new home.
  Deleting it was better than any of those homes. It landed as its own commit,
  so `git revert 52f5b14` brings it back if "for now" ever expires.
- **The three.js lab** — see §7. It no longer even runs.
- **The session intro-skip** — you have decided to remove it. §7 shows how to
  keep the useful half of what it built.

The test to apply to every future addition: *if this is removed in a year, will
anyone notice?* If not, it is a feature that exists because it was easy.

### 0.6 The standing test

At every phase, **take a screenshot at rest and ask whether it still looks like a
photograph of an instrument.** If new UI is the first thing your eye lands on,
back it off. Then open a panel and ask the harder version: **does the panel look
like it belongs to the same object as the watch?** Today, honestly, it does not.

## 1. A note on jargon

You asked what "IA" meant. Fair — it was jargon, and jargon in a document meant
to be argued with is a failure of the document, not of the reader. Writing that
hides behind vocabulary is its own kind of slop: it looks like expertise and
functions as fog.

So this draft explains its terms where they appear. A short glossary of the ones
that recur, all in plain language:

- **Information architecture (IA)** — what sections the site has, what goes in
  each one, and what they are called. Nothing more. If you wrote the site out as
  a table of contents on one sheet of paper, that list is the information
  architecture. §5 is now titled "What sections the site has" for that reason.
- **Deep link** — a link that opens a *specific part* of a site rather than its
  front door. `…/work` instead of `…`. §7 covers this at length.
- **Routing** — the code that decides which part of the site to show based on
  what the address bar says.
- **Media query** — a line of CSS that only applies below or above a certain
  screen width. It is how a layout changes shape on a phone. The site currently
  has none that do this.
- **Viewport** — the visible area of the browser window, in pixels.
- **`vmin`** — a CSS unit meaning "1% of whichever is smaller, the window's width
  or its height." The whole site is sized in it, which is why everything scales
  together and nothing ever reflows.
- **Affordance** — the visible hint that tells you something can be clicked.
- **`z-index`** — which layer sits in front of which.
- **State** — the small object in `main.js` recording what is currently true
  (which panel is open, whether the watch is flipped). Everything drawn on screen
  is drawn from it.

Watch terminology (rehaut, hallmark, anglage, AUF/AB) is left alone. That is your
domain, not the fog.

## 2. What is actually there today

Worth being precise about, because most of the recommendations fall out of it.
Several findings from the first draft have since been fixed by other work; those
are marked **[fixed]** so this section can be trusted as current.

### The shell

`.stage` is a flex column, `min-height:100vh`, `overflow:hidden`, centred.
Inside: `.loader`, then `.watch-stage` (`86vmin × 86vmin`, `perspective: 2400px`),
then `.caption` (`height:5vmin`), then `.hint-bar` (absolutely positioned,
`bottom:2.6vmin`, `font-size:1.05vmin`), then the `.overlay` panel layer
(`position:fixed; z-index:40`). The `.req-layer` modal used to sit above that at
`z-index:60`; it has since been deleted, so 40 is now the top of the stack.

### The state machine

`src/main.js` is better structured than it looks. `state` is a flat object,
`render()` paints *every* piece of UI from it — pose, flip, hands, panels, hints
— and it is idempotent. It runs from `setInterval(render, 100)` plus a direct
call on every event handler, with `requestAnimationFrame` borrowed for the
duration of the demonstration sweep and handed back.

That one-way `state → render()` flow is the single most valuable thing in the
codebase for this project. **A navigation index is not new machinery. It is a
second view of `state.active`, painted by the same function that already paints
the panels.** Every recommendation about keeping navigation in sync in this
document is that sentence, elaborated.

### The five bindings

```js
bind($('aboutHit'),    'about');     // hours dial
bind($('dateWindow'),  'featured');  // outsize date
bind($('secondsDial'), 'currently'); // small seconds
bind($('reserve'),     'resume');    // power reserve
bind($('moon'),        'books');     // moonphase
```

`#crown` (flip) and `#corrector` (the demonstration sweep) are wired by hand
alongside.

### Parallel lists describing the same five things

`CAPTIONS`, `ZOOM_ORIGINS`, the five `bind()` calls, and the literal text of
`#hintBar`. Change one section and you edit four places in two files. This is
still the first thing to fix and it is invisible to the user.

One nuance the first draft got wrong: the five `.hint-mark` shapes in
`index.html` are **measured geometry** (`cx="31.797" cy="49.999" r="25.299"` and
so on, tracing the real complications), exactly as `ZOOM_ORIGINS` is. Those two
should sit *next to* the section list, not be folded into it — they are derived
from the art and belong with the art, per §0.2.

### Measured in the browser, current tree

**At 1440×900, nothing open:** watch is 774px across, occupying y 29→803.
Caption sits y 826→871. Horizontal margin either side: **333px**. Vertical
margin, all in: **58px**, and the caption already owns most of the bottom.

**At 1440×900 with a panel open:** the posed watch (`translateX(-28vw) scale(.72)`)
spans **x 30 → 594**. The card spans x 569 → 1395. The composition works — this
is what the design intends. Note the number 30: see §4, it decides the whole
navigation question.

**At 900×760 with a panel open:** the card is 826px wide (720px of content plus
52px padding each side plus the border — `.card` is content-box) inside a 900px
window. The watch is a blurred gold arc in the top-left corner. **The "watch
stays visible beside the panel" promise only holds north of about 1400px**, as
the first draft said. Confirmed unchanged.

**At 390×800 (a phone):**

- `.hint-bar` computes to **4.1px**. `.caption` computes to **5.3px**. Both are
  below the size at which text is text.
- `.cb-hallmark` on the caseback computes to **3.8px**.
- The watch is 335px, sitting between **218px of empty space above and 247px
  below**. Centring a square in a tall window wastes nearly 60% of the screen.
  There is *more* than enough room for a real mobile index; it is simply not used.
- The open panel is 346 × 732, effectively a full-screen sheet whose only close
  affordance is `ESC ✕` — and a phone has no Escape key. Browser Back exits the
  site.

**Media queries:** two exist. `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` and
`@media (hover: hover)`. **Neither changes the layout at any width.** The first
draft said "no media queries at all"; that is now inaccurate, but the substantive
finding stands: *the layout does not reflow, on any device.*

**Keyboard:** still nothing. All five hit targets are `div`s with no `role` and
no `tabindex`. Zero `tabindex` attributes exist in the file. **No content on this
site is reachable by keyboard.** This is a correctness bug, not a nicety, and it
is cheap to fix right now — see §8, Phase 0. It gets expensive later.

**Hover can stick:** if the pointer leaves a hit target without firing
`mouseleave` (a tab-out, a scroll, a fast jump), the caption keeps the old text.
Minor today; it gets worse the moment navigation also writes `state.hover`.

### Already fixed since the first draft

- **[fixed] The pulsing hint dots are gone.** Replaced by `.hint-index`: five
  hairline SVG marks tracing the real complication geometry, which scribe
  themselves in sequence once at `--hint-at: 4.11s` and then stop. Stroked with
  `url(#hintKey)`, a single 315° gradient in `userSpaceOnUse` across the whole
  face. This is a better answer than the "index sweep" the first draft proposed
  and it should be considered done.
- **[fixed] The two competing hover vocabularies are unified.** The three
  `box-shadow: 0 0 0 .25vmin` outline rules are gone. Every hit target now takes
  light — `filter: brightness(1.16) saturate(1.04)`, or for the hours dial a
  radial wash centred at 32%/30% where the dial's own key already falls. The CSS
  comment states the principle exactly right: *"Line says which parts are live;
  light says which one you are on. Two channels, no collision."* Adopt that
  sentence as a rule; it is the clearest statement of the site's visual grammar
  anywhere in the tree.
- **[fixed] The 10 o'clock corrector has a job again.** It runs the
  demonstration sweep. It no longer opens the review form.
- **[new] The intro is now ~4.11s**, not 3.7s — the movement act moved in front
  of the watch build. `--hint-at` is the one number that follows it.

### Currently broken

`npx vite` prints a dependency-scan failure on every start, because
`src/lab3d.js` imports `three` and `three` is **in `package.json` but not
installed**. The 3D lab cannot run at all. See §7.

## 4. Navigation — and the top bar, re-argued

You asked: *"What if we do a top bar?"* The first draft argued for a left
vertical rail and gave three reasons. You are right to push on it, and having
re-measured with "permanent hero" locked, **the first draft's conclusion was
right for the wrong reason, and its own proposal turns out to be broken.**

Here is the argument from scratch.

### 4.1 The strongest case for a top bar

Stated as well as I can, because it is better than the first draft allowed:

1. **It is unmissable.** A left rail at 55% opacity in the margin is
   discoverable-ish. A top bar is where every visitor on earth already looks. For
   a portfolio whose most valuable visitor is a recruiter giving it twenty
   seconds, "where do I click" being answered instantly has real, unglamorous
   value.
2. **Permanent hero removes the top bar's main source of fussiness.** This is the
   genuinely new argument your decision creates. Because nothing scrolls, a top
   bar never needs shrink-on-scroll, hide-on-scroll-down, sticky/unsticky states,
   or a scroll-position highlight. It is a static strip that never moves. All the
   things that make top bars fiddly to build and easy to get subtly wrong simply
   do not apply here.
3. **It leaves the horizontal axis alone.** The site's central composition is
   watch-left / panel-right. A left rail inserts a third column into a two-column
   relationship. A top bar does not touch it.
4. **The wordmark objection is answerable.** A top bar's canonical left slot is a
   logo, which would repeat `.maker-name` — but you can simply leave the left
   slot empty and range the items centre or right. A bar with no logo is not the
   template shape.
5. **One design instead of two.** A left rail has to become something else
   entirely below ~720px — the first draft conceded it turns into a bottom bar.
   That is two designs for one job. By §0.4's own rule, fewer idioms is better.

Points 2 and 5 are strong, and 5 in particular is an argument the first draft
made *against itself* without noticing.

### 4.2 What the measurements say

**The vertical budget is not scarce. It is already spent.**

At 1440×900: the watch is 774px and occupies y 29→803. The caption occupies
826→871. Total unallocated vertical space, top and bottom combined: **58px.**

`.stage` is a centred flex column with `overflow: hidden`. Add a 44px bar as a
child and the column grows past the viewport, and because it is centred, the
overflow is split — **the case gets clipped by about 22px at the top and 22px at
the bottom.** A visible flat edge on a circular gold case is not a subtle problem.

To avoid that, the watch must shrink. `86vmin → 80vmin` at 1440×900 buys the 54px
a bar needs and costs the watch **7% of its diameter** — on a site whose entire
proposition is the watch. That is the real price of a top bar, and it is not
recoverable by cleverness; the axis is simply full.

**But the left rail is worse, and this is the finding that decides it.**

At 1440×900 *with a panel open*, the posed watch spans **x 30 → 594**. The left
margin is 30px, not 333px. A 156px rail pinned to the left edge would sit **on
top of the case for 126px of its width** — violating the first draft's own rule 4
("nothing overlaps the case circle") in the exact state where navigation matters
most. The grid-shell fix it proposed does not save this either: the pose is
`translateX(-28vw)`, and `vw` is measured against the whole window, not against a
grid column, so the watch pushes into the rail regardless.

**The left margin only exists when nothing is open — that is, only when you don't
need the navigation.**

So: the top bar costs the watch 7% of its diameter, and the left rail is
structurally broken. Neither is the answer.

### 4.3 Recommendation: a bottom index bar, built by promoting `#hintBar`

The site already has one. `.hint-bar` sits at `bottom: 2.6vmin`, full width, in
IBM Plex Mono at `.24em` tracking, and reads:

```
DIAL · DATE · SECONDS · RESERVE · MOON — CLICK TO EXPLORE  ·  CROWN TO FLIP
```

**That is already a five-item index with a mode line, rendered as inert 4px
text.** Making it live is the smallest diff available and the largest effect,
and it answers every point in §4.1 except the first:

- **Zero layout cost.** It is already positioned, already in the vertical budget,
  already in the right typeface at the right tracking and colour. The watch does
  not shrink by a single pixel.
- **It does not collide with the watch in any state.** It is full-width and below
  the case in both the resting and posed compositions. Verified at 1440×900,
  900×760 and 390×800.
- **One design, every viewport.** On desktop it reads as a printed scale beneath
  an instrument. On a phone it is a bottom tab bar — the native convention, in
  thumb reach. Same element, same markup, same idiom; only the type size changes.
  This is §0.4 satisfied rather than argued about.
- **It sits with the caption.** `.caption` is directly above it and already says
  what you are pointing at. Index and readout become one zone: one place to look,
  which is exactly how an instrument's printed scale and its pointer relate.
- **No logo slot exists**, so the wordmark can never be repeated. The problem
  disappears rather than being managed.
- **Permanent hero makes it static.** Nothing scrolls, so it never hides, shrinks
  or re-sticks. The same benefit the top bar gets from your decision, without the
  7%.

The one thing a top bar wins on is raw conventionality, and a full-width strip
directly under the object of interest, adjacent to the line that is already
narrating that object, is not hard to find.

### 4.4 Shape

Seven slots, in one row, in the vocabulary that already exists:

```
ABOUT · WORK · CURRENTLY · EXPERIENCE · READING · CONTACT        CROWN TO FLIP
```

- Separator is the existing ` · `, already used by `#hintBar` and `.cb-hallmark`.
- Type: `--font-mono`, `.24em` tracking, `#5b6172` at rest — all current values.
- The active item is marked by a **short gold hairline directly beneath it** —
  a pointer against a scale, the same gesture the reserve hand makes against
  AUF/AB. Not a pill, not a fill, not a colour swap, not an underline that spans
  the word.
- The hovered item **takes light**: it brightens toward `--gold-line-hover`. This
  is the rule the CSS already states — *line says which parts are live, light says
  which one you are on* — applied to the chrome. It means the index and the watch
  are hovered in the same language.
- No icons, no boxes, no border-radius, no background fill, no blur panel.

### 4.5 The five changes it needs

1. **Stay visible when a panel is open.** Today `render()` does
   `el.hintBar.hidden = !(CONFIG.showHints && !state.active && !state.reqOpen)`
   — it hides. Persistent navigation must persist. `z-index: 50`: above the
   `.overlay` (40) so you can move section to section without closing first.
2. **A minimum type size.** `1.05vmin` is 9.5px at 1440×900 and **4.1px on a
   phone**. `font-size: clamp(10px, 1.15vmin, 12.5px)`. `.caption` needs the same
   treatment (`5.3px` on a phone today).
3. **Real hit targets.** The ink is ~10px; each item gets transparent vertical
   padding to a ≥28px box. Invisible on desktop, essential on touch.
4. **Real elements.** Each item becomes an `<a href="#/about">` (see §7) with an
   `aria-label`, so keyboard and screen-reader access come free rather than being
   retrofitted.
5. **Painted from `state`.** `render()` sets `.is-on` from `state.active` and
   `.is-cued` from `state.hover`. No new machinery — it is one more block in the
   function that already paints everything else.

### 4.6 The reciprocal cue — the hinge of the whole plan

**Hovering a bar item lights the corresponding watch part, and hovering a watch
part lights the bar item.** `state.hover` already drives `CAPTIONS`; extend it to
also toggle `.is-cued` on the bound node, reusing the exact light treatment the
`@media (hover: hover)` block already defines.

This is what makes the bar worth building. A first-time visitor who does the
conventional thing — read the nav, click a word — is shown, without a tooltip or
a line of instruction, that **the watch is the same navigation.** The bar teaches
the watch. Which means the watch's own signalling (`.hint-index`) is free to stay
as quiet and as one-shot as it already is.

On touch, where there is no hover: tapping a bar item cues its watch part for
~600ms *before* the panel opens. Same mechanism, on a timer. That is how a phone
visitor learns the mapping, and it is the only way they ever will.

### 4.7 If you want the top bar anyway

It is your site and conventionality is a real virtue. If you choose it, these are
the terms that make it work rather than making it a template:

- **Height: 44px fixed**, in `px` not `vmin`, so it does not grow on large
  screens.
- **The watch must stop clipping.** `.watch-stage` becomes
  `width: min(86vmin, calc(100vh - 44px - 9vmin)); height: <same>` — the `9vmin`
  is the caption block. Accept that the watch is now ~7% smaller at 1440×900.
- **No fill, no border, no blur.** The bar is text on the page background. At
  most one gold hairline beneath it at very low opacity, and try it without one
  first.
- **Left slot empty.** Items ranged right or centred. Never a wordmark, never a
  logo — `.maker-name` is the wordmark and it is on the dial.
- **No scroll behaviour of any kind.** Nothing scrolls; the bar is inert.
- **`#hintBar` must be deleted, not kept.** Index text at the top *and* the
  bottom is the crowding failure. Its mode line ("CROWN TO FLIP") moves into the
  caption.
- **Mobile: it becomes the bottom bar anyway.** Accept that you are maintaining
  two arrangements of the same list.

My recommendation remains the bottom bar, and the deciding reason is §4.2: the
vertical axis is already fully spent, and the bottom is the only place a
persistent index can go without either shrinking the watch or sitting on it.

## 5. What sections the site has

*(This section used to be called "Information architecture." It means: what
sections exist, what goes in each, what they are called. Nothing more.)*

### 5.1 The constraint nobody named

Every section is currently pinned to a complication. That is charming, and it is
also a ceiling: adding a section means finding a watch part for it, and a Lange 1
has about seven clickable features (dial, date, reserve, small seconds, moonphase,
crown, corrector) before you start inventing hardware — which `HANDOFF.md`
explicitly forbids.

**Recommendation: decouple them.** The bar is the complete list; the watch carries
the five it can express. Sections without a complication simply appear in the bar
and cue nothing on the dial. Signal the asymmetry honestly: bar items that own a
complication show their marker in gold, those that do not show it in grey. Small,
legible rule; the section list can grow without the watch having to.

But hold this next line above it, because §0.1 outranks everything:

> **Seven complications is a ceiling, not a target.** A section that exists to
> fill a slot is worse than an empty slot. If there is no real Writing yet, there
> is no Writing section.

### 5.2 Proposed sections

### 5.3 "Currently" — what was actually being asked

You wrote *"Explain?"* against this one. Here is the question in full.

**What exists now.** `CURRENTLY` is a four-item list in `main.js` that rotates
every four seconds. It is *already displayed continuously* in the caption line
under the watch — right now the caption reads `CURRENTLY READING — LOREM IPSUM: A
HISTORY` and cycles. Clicking the small seconds opens a card whose entire content
is that same one line, larger. **So the click costs you an interaction and gives
you nothing you weren't already reading.** It is the thinnest section on the site
and it occupies a full navigation slot.

**Option A — make it a real section: a running log.** The card holds the last
8–12 entries, each dated. *What it costs:* you have to keep writing it, forever.
*What it risks:* a "Currently" whose newest entry says June when it is December is
the single clearest sign of an abandoned personal site — worse than not having
one at all. *What it's worth:* it is the most alive idea on the site, it is the
cheapest real content that exists (one line a month), and it genuinely earns the
small seconds, which is the hand that never stops.

**Option B — fold it into About.** The rotating line stays in the caption
(nothing is lost — it already lives there), the small seconds cues About instead,
and the section list drops by one. *What it costs:* almost nothing. *What it
loses:* the one part of the site that would prove a person is still here.

**Recommendation: A, with a rule that makes it impossible to embarrass you.**

Every entry carries a date. **If the newest entry is more than ~8 weeks old, the
section does not render at all** — it drops out of the bar and the small seconds
cues About instead, exactly as in Option B. About twelve lines of code.

This is worth the trouble because it converts the decision from "will you
maintain this forever?" — which nobody can honestly answer — into a design that
degrades gracefully when you don't. A stale section is slop; a section that
quietly stands down when it goes stale is not. And it is *self-defending*, which
is the same instinct as the rest of the codebase.

### 5.4 Work needs a second level

Three lines of text is a list of things you did, not a portfolio. A real personal
site needs one project you can read for two minutes.

Pattern: the card holds the list; clicking a row expands the detail **in place**
within the same card (push/pop), keeping the watch pose. No second overlay layer,
no change to the composition. It does raise the linking question — a project worth
writing about is a project worth linking to — see §7.

Sequencing: **do not build this before the writing exists.** A detail view
containing more lorem is a worse outcome than no detail view.

### 5.5 Content should stop living in markup

All copy is hand-authored inside a 524-line `index.html`. Adding project detail
means much more of it.

Recommendation: move **panel content only** into data (`src/content.js`) rendered
by a small template function. The watch markup stays exactly as it is, untouched.
Two reasons beyond tidiness: writing prose is much easier in a file that is prose
than in a file that is 33KB of generated rig markup; and while several agents are
editing the watch, new content in a new file is the lowest-merge-risk shape
available.

## 6. Contact on the caseback

Settled: contact lives on the caseback, and a CONTACT control in the bar flips
the watch there in addition to the crown. Your note: *"There will be all of the
contact info and links, etc. Where and how is tbd."* So — here is the where and
the how.

### 6.1 The surface, measured

I flipped the watch and measured it. At 1440×900 (case = 774px across):

| Element | Geometry |
| --- | --- |
| `.cb-band` | full face, 774px |
| `.cb-ring` | `inset: 9.5%` → outer radius 313.5px |
| `.cb-aperture` | `inset: 12%` — the sapphire window |
| `.cb-hallmark` | `bottom: 3.2%`, `.98vmin` (8.8px here) |
| Band arcs | `#cbRightArc` / `#cbLeftArc`, text centred at 3 and 9 o'clock |

Two findings that decide the design:

**1. The aperture is full and it is the best thing on the site.** Once the rig
loads, the movement fills the window — plate engraving, `VIERZIG (40) RUBINE`,
`N. ROSHAN RAMLUKAN / GLASHÜTTE I/SA`, `Nº 58669`, gold chatons, blued screws,
the balance beating. **Nothing goes inside the sapphire. Ever.** That is the
non-negotiable of this section.

**2. The top and bottom of the band are free.** The two engraved arcs run from
top-centre to bottom-centre, and with `startOffset="50%"` their text is centred at
3 o'clock and 9 o'clock. So the band's **top and bottom lenses carry no
engraving.** Each is ~73px tall at the centreline and, measured as a chord,
**~435px wide** about 40px below the ring's lowest point, narrowing to ~227px at
the case edge.

The bottom lens holds `.cb-hallmark` — 8.8px of it, out of 73px available. **The
band has a large, empty, already-idiomatic engraving register that nothing uses.**
That is where contact goes.

### 6.2 The design

**One engraved line in the bottom lens, directly above the hallmark.**

```
              EMAIL · GITHUB · LINKEDIN · CV
                Au 750 · Nº 1 · 250735
```

Six decisions, each with a reason:

**a. Words, not addresses.** `EMAIL`, not `nikhil.ramlukan@gmail.com`. A caseback
bears marks, not paragraphs — that is what makes it a caseback and not a footer.
Four words at 11px with `.28em` tracking measure ~300px against 435px of lens; the
address would not fit and would look like a form field engraved on gold.

**b. The address is read out in the caption.** `.caption` already sits directly
beneath the watch, already in mono, already driven by `state.hover`, and already
exists to say what you are pointing at. Hovering or focusing EMAIL puts
`NIKHIL.RAMLUKAN@GMAIL.COM` in it — full size, legible, selectable, copyable. This
is `CAPTIONS` with five more entries; **no new machinery, and no `mailto:`-only
dead end** for the visitor who wants to copy an address rather than launch a mail
client.

**c. It is engraving, not UI.** At rest: `.cb-engraved`'s own treatment —
`fill: #3f2009`, `opacity: .8`, one weight up in size from the hallmark so it
correctly reads as the more important of the two lines. On hover or focus, the
line **takes the light**: stroked or filled from the `#hintKey` gradient the
interaction index already uses (`userSpaceOnUse`, one 315° ramp across the whole
face). Reusing that exact gradient is the point — it means the contact line is lit
by the same key as the case, the dial furniture and the index marks, so it reads
as an edge of metal rather than as a hyperlink. And it obeys the site's stated
rule: *light says which one you are on.*

**d. The top lens stays empty.** Deliberately. An empty upper band is what lets
the eye land on the movement first. Filling both lenses would frame the sapphire
in text and turn a watch back into a plaque.

**e. Real anchors.** `<a href="mailto:…">`, `<a href="https://github.com/…"
rel="noopener">`, inside a `.cb-contact` layer on `.face-back`. Keyboard
reachability and screen-reader labelling come free. Note `.face` already carries
`backface-visibility: hidden`, which stops the browser hit-testing the back face
while the watch is front-on — **so the links cannot be clicked through the dial.**
Verified in the CSS; the builder does not need to solve this.

**f. Touch targets.** The ink is 11px; each link gets transparent vertical padding
to a ~28px box, and the whole register gets `pointer-events: auto` (its
neighbours — `.cb-band-svg`, `.cb-movement`, `.cb-hallmark` — are all
`pointer-events: none` today, so the register is the only interactive thing back
there, which is exactly right).

### 6.3 The flip, when a nav control triggers it

**The bar's CONTACT item presses the crown.**

On activation, `#crown` takes the same depressed state a real click gives it for
~160ms, and *then* the flip starts. Those 160ms are not latency — they are the
causal chain being drawn. Without them, a click at the bottom of the screen makes
an object in the middle of the screen spin, and the visitor does not know why.
With them, they watch the crown go in and the watch turn over.

It also **teaches the crown**, which is the §4.6 principle applied to the seventh
control: someone who reaches contact from the bar is shown what the crown does,
and next time they use the crown. One idea, extended — not a second idea.

### 6.4 Arrival: no second mode

**The contact register is always engraved, however you arrive.** There is no
"contact mode" of the caseback.

Two reasons. Contact information hidden behind a mode is a defect — someone
exploring the caseback out of curiosity should find the address. And one surface
with one appearance is one fewer thing to design, explain, and get subtly wrong.

The *only* difference is where your attention is taken:

- **Arrived via CONTACT:** the existing `cbRigSheen` mechanism replays once, ~0.9s,
  translated to run along the bottom lens rather than across the movement. Your
  eye follows it to the register. One pass, then nothing.
- **Arrived via the crown:** no sheen. You look at the movement, exactly as today.
  The register is quietly there at the bottom, in the same way the hallmark is
  quietly there now.

That is a one-shot motion that explains where to look — permitted under §0.3 —
rather than an ambient state change.

### 6.5 The way back

The sharpest question in this section, because a flipped watch is a place you can
get stuck. Four exits, all of which must work:

1. **The crown.** Unchanged. The caption already says `CLICK THE CROWN TO TURN
   BACK` while flipped; keep it, overridden by the address on hover.
2. **Browser Back.** If `#/contact` is a real address (§7), popping it returns to
   `#/` and unflips. **This is the real fix on a phone**, which has no Escape key
   and where Back currently leaves the site.
3. **Escape.** Today `Escape` clears `state.active` and `state.reqOpen` only. If
   the caseback is a destination, **Escape must also clear `state.flipped`.** One
   line, and it removes the "I'm stuck on the back" case entirely.
4. **Any other bar item while flipped.** This must not open a panel over the
   caseback. Sequence: set `state.flipped = false` and `state.active = <id>` in
   the same tick, and let the two existing transitions run **concurrently** —
   `.watch-flip` is `transform 1.15s`, `.watch-pose` is `transform 1s`. Delay the
   card's own `cardUp` entrance to ~0.6s so the content arrives once the watch is
   past 90° and reading as a dial again. Total ~1.2s, which is the flip's existing
   cost and not a new one. Doing it in sequence — unflip fully, then pose, then
   open — is 2.2s and feels like standing in a queue.

### 6.6 Mobile: an honest degradation

The engraving does not survive a phone, and pretending otherwise would be the
kind of thing this document exists to prevent. At 390×800 the watch is 335px, so
the bottom lens is ~32px tall and ~190px wide, and `.cb-hallmark` computes to
**3.8px**. Four engraved words are not going to happen.

So:

- **The flip still happens.** The movement is the best thing on the site and a
  phone visitor should absolutely see it.
- **The links do not go on the band.** Tapping CONTACT flips the watch **and**
  swaps the bottom bar's row to `EMAIL · GITHUB · LINKEDIN · CV`, at the bar's own
  real px size with the bar's own tap targets. Tapping CONTACT again, or any other
  item, flips back and restores the index.
- **The band keeps only the hallmark**, exactly as today.

The rule to remember: **the flip is universal; the engraving is a desktop
refinement.** This keeps one vocabulary rather than inventing a mobile contact
sheet, because the bar is already the phone's primary navigation — the links
arrive in the place the visitor is already looking.

### 6.7 What this replaces

The first draft proposed contact as "a second engraved arc on the band." That is
now measurably wrong: the two arcs already occupy the left and right of the band
and a third would crowd them. The bottom lens is both emptier and better — it is
where a caseback already puts its identifying marks, and an address is the same
kind of mark as a serial number.

## 7. Addresses, links, and the intro

### 7.1 Routing and deep links — what is actually being asked

You wrote *"Explain more"* here, and the first draft called this the biggest
structural decision. It is, so here it is properly.

**The situation today.** The address bar always says the same thing. Open About,
open Experience, flip the watch — the address never changes. There is exactly one
address for the entire site.

**What that costs, concretely:**

- **You cannot send anyone a link to your CV.** You can only send them to the
  front door and tell them which part of the watch to click. For a portfolio,
  "cannot link to my own CV" is close to a defect.
- **The browser's Back button does not close a panel.** On a phone this is bad:
  Back leaves the site entirely, and a phone has no Escape key, so the `ESC ✕`
  label names a key the device does not have.
- **Reloading always returns you to the front door** — and now, with the session
  memory removed, through the full 4.11s intro again.
- **One page means one title and one description.** Google indexes one thing.
  There is no "Nikhil Ramlukan — Experience" result.

**The cheap version: hash links.** The address gains a suffix — `…/#/about`,
`…/#/experience`, `…/#/contact`. Bookmarkable, shareable, Back works, reload lands
where you were. About fifteen lines:

```js
function open(id) {
  state.active = id; state.touched = true; state.hover = null;
  history.pushState(null, '', id ? '#/' + id : location.pathname);
  render();
}
addEventListener('popstate', () => {
  state.active = idFromHash(location.hash);   // null → closed
  render();
});
```

The `#` part of a URL is never sent to the server, so nothing about hosting,
building or deployment changes. `#/contact` also sets `state.flipped`.

**What hash links do not buy.** When you paste the link into Slack, LinkedIn,
WhatsApp or an email, the preview card shows the *site's* title and image — not
the section's. Because underneath, there is still only one HTML page. Same for
search results.

**The expensive version: real addresses.** `nikhilramlukan.com/work/…`, with a
separate pre-built HTML file per section so each carries its own title,
description and preview image. That is what "real URLs" buys beyond the cheap
version, and it is the only thing it buys: **per-section previews and per-section
search results.** The cost is that the site stops being one hand-authored
`index.html` — which `HANDOFF.md` calls the source of truth — and becomes a build
with several entry points. Roughly 1–2 days, plus permanent complexity in a
project whose simplicity is a feature.

**One rule if you ever do it: the watch must not reload between sections.** A
watch that re-assembles for four seconds every time someone clicks a nav item
would destroy the thing that makes this site worth visiting. So real addresses
have to be built as "one app, several pre-rendered entry files," never as separate
pages.

**Recommendation: hash links now (Phase 3). Real addresses only if and when a
single piece of writing or a single project deserves its own preview card.**

**Re-posed as a question you can answer:** *Do you expect to paste a link to one
specific project or essay into a message or a job application, and want it to
preview with that project's own title and picture — rather than with the site's?*
If no, hash links are the whole answer. If yes, that is Phase 7, and it should
happen only after the writing exists to justify it.

### 7.2 The deep-link problem, and the gift the intro-skip leaves behind

Someone opening a shared `#/experience` link must not sit through 4.11 seconds of
assembly. That is the one fiddly part of either version.

The mechanism already exists — and here is the pleasing part. The
`sessionStorage` intro-skip you are removing built exactly this:

```css
html[data-intro-seen] .loader,
html[data-intro-seen] .watch-mvt { display: none !important; }
html[data-intro-seen] .stage *:not(.hint-mark):not(.cb-rig-sheen) { animation-delay: 0s !important; }
```

Zeroing only the *delays*, with the two staggered sequences excluded so they keep
their own timing. That is a careful, correct piece of work, and it is exactly what
a deep link needs.

**Recommendation: delete the inline `sessionStorage` script, keep these three CSS
rules, and repoint them.** Rename the attribute (`data-direct`, say) and set it
before first paint when `location.hash` is non-empty. A deletion becomes a
foundation rather than a loss. Budget half a day to get the loader, the
`rig-ready` promise and the flip transition all behaving; it is well understood
but it has corners.

### 7.3 No first-visit memory — the honest cost

Settled: the same experience every time, no `sessionStorage`, no `localStorage`.
This is the right instinct — a site that behaves differently for people it
recognises is harder to reason about, and "identical every time" is a defensible
position that also happens to be simpler.

**The cost, stated plainly: every visit plays the full ~4.11s intro before the
watch is usable.** Including a reload. Including the visitor who came back for a
second look — who is, by definition, the most engaged person to ever open the
site. 4.11 seconds is long. Watch for it.

**If it ever grates, the fix is not to reintroduce memory.** It is to make the
intro *interruptible*: any click, tap or keypress during assembly jumps
immediately to the finished state, using the same delay-collapse rule from §7.2.
That is stateless, it gives everyone the identical experience by default, and it
respects a returning visitor without remembering them. Not a decision to make now
— just the escape hatch, recorded, so nobody reaches for `localStorage` in six
months.

Two consequences worth noting while the removal happens: `.hint-index` is gated on
`state.touched`, which never persists, so **the interaction index now scribes
itself on every single visit.** That is a slightly louder site than before, and it
is the correct trade given the decision — the index is one-shot and quiet, and
without memory it is the only thing telling a returning visitor what is clickable.
And the intro-skip's CSS should be preserved per §7.2 rather than deleted with it.

## 8. The three.js lab, and how depth folds in

`docs/3D-ASSESSMENT.md` landed after the first draft of this plan. Its findings
are good and they should not sit in a separate document; here is the merge.

### 8.1 Delete the lab — it is now housekeeping, not a judgement call

The assessment recommended deleting `lab-3d.html`, `src/lab3d.js`,
`src/movement-3d.json`, the `three` dependency and the two retired SVG movement
generators, and it framed the delete-vs-quarantine choice as worth thirty seconds
of your opinion.

**It still is — an earlier draft of this section was wrong.** It claimed the lab
no longer runs, on the evidence that `three` was missing from `node_modules` and
`npx vite` failed its dependency scan. That was a local install state, not a
property of the project: `three` is still declared in `package.json`, so a fresh
`npm install` restores it. Checked directly in a tree where it is installed,
`/lab-3d.html` and `/src/lab3d.js` both serve 200 and vite starts clean.

So this stays a judgment call, not housekeeping. The case for deleting is
unchanged and still good: the lab renders a visibly worse movement than the
sprite caseback it duplicates, it has not been touched in 110 commits, and
keeping it signals that three.js is a sanctioned direction when `HANDOFF.md:33`
says it is not. The case against is only that it costs nothing while nothing
imports it. Decide on the merits above; do not decide because it appears broken.
Whichever way, rewrite `HANDOFF.md:33` to record *why* so the question is not
re-litigated in six months. Git
keeps everything: `git show f2c7e72:src/lab3d.js`.

This is §0.5 in action. It is the cleanest available example of deleting being
progress.

### 8.2 Pointer tilt — where it belongs and on what terms

The assessment's recommendation was pointer-driven tilt on the existing CSS rig
(±8–12°, ~half a day), plus giving the outsize date its aperture depth and
lifting the hands off the dial. It tested this and found the rig already looks
convincingly three-dimensional at 24°.

**It belongs in a late phase, not an early one** (Phase 5 below). It is polish on
an object; it changes nothing about what the site *is*, and §0.1 says what the
site is comes first.

Four of its six open questions can be closed now, from this document's rules:

- **Does tilt violate "no ambient floating"?** No — *provided* it is pointer-driven
  only, has no idle drift, has no CSS transition (rAF damping instead, or it will
  lag behind the pointer badly), and settles to **exactly zero** at rest. Under
  those terms it explains depth, which is the permitted category. Any version that
  moves on its own is out.
- **Should tilt stay active while a panel is open?** **No.** The watch is context
  at that point, not the subject, and an object gently tilting behind a card that
  someone is reading is precisely the ambient motion the design bible bans.
- **Touch devices?** **Nothing.** No `deviceorientation`. A permission prompt for a
  decorative tilt is a bad trade, and it would be the site's first permission
  dialog. Tilt is a desktop refinement; the flip remains the touch interaction.
- **Delete or quarantine the lab?** Delete. §8.1.

The clamp angle stays genuinely yours — it is an aesthetic limit (at what angle
does the baked sunburst dial stop reading as brushed metal and start reading as a
printed sticker?), not a technical one. Start at ±10°, make it a single CSS
variable, judge it on a screenshot at maximum tilt with the dial's bright axis
pointing the wrong way.

## 9. Rules for not looking like a template

The failure mode is specific, so name it and hold the line. **The first draft
applied these to the navigation chrome only. They apply to the panels first** —
that is where a visitor spends all of their reading time, and per §0.4 it is where
the site is currently weakest.

### For the panels

1. **The card must belong to the same object as the watch.** Today it does not:
   `border-radius: 6px`, a 1px border, a gradient fill and a 90px drop shadow are
   vocabulary that exists nowhere on the case. Rework it toward the watch's own
   materials — the dial's ink navy, a hairline at `--gold-line`, light from 315°,
   and *no* radius that does not already exist in the file.
2. **No pills, no chips, no bordered tags.** `.tag` is four bordered rounded
   rectangles. On a site whose visual grammar is hairline rules and specular light,
   they read as a component library. Set them as mono micro-caps separated by the
   ` · ` the site already uses, or as ticks against a rule.
3. **Type from the three approved faces only.** Marcellus for display, Archivo for
   body, IBM Plex Mono for technical labels. That is already true; keep it true.
4. **The panel is a page of a manual, not a modal.** No scrim blur, no bounce, no
   scale-in from 0.9. `cardUp` is fine; keep it restrained.

### For the chrome

5. **The dial is the wordmark.** `.maker-name` sets NIKHIL RAMLUKAN in Marcellus
   at generous tracking. The chrome must never repeat the name.
6. **The chrome is drawn from the watch's vocabulary.** Hairline rules at
   `--gold-line`, IBM Plex Mono micro-caps at `.24em`, the 315° key light. No
   boxes, no pills, no filled buttons, no icon set.
7. **Nothing overlaps the case circle in any state** — including the posed state,
   where the watch reaches to x=30 at 1440×900. This is what killed the left rail.
8. **Chrome is absent during assembly.** Nothing appears before ~4.1s, matching
   `.hint-bar`'s current `animation: fadeIn .8s ease 4.01s`. The first thing a
   visitor sees is a watch being built, with no UI around it.
9. **Chrome recedes.** At rest the bar is `#5b6172` micro-caps. Never a hard-edged
   panel, never a fill.
10. **The bar is a scale, not a menu.** A pointer against index marks, reading like
    the AUF/AB scale already on the dial. If it ever looks like a row of links, it
    is wrong.

### For the watch

Unchanged and non-negotiable: do not touch sprite geometry, the measured ratios in
`docs/DESIGN_BIBLE.md`, `ZOOM_ORIGINS`, the `.hint-mark` coordinates, the `.cb-rig`
block (generated — never hand-edited), the assembly order, or anything under
`tools/`. Add the index bar, the contact register and the tilt clamp to the
regression checklist in `docs/HANDOFF.md` when they land.

## 10. Phasing

Each phase is independently shippable. The big change from the first draft: **real
content moved from Phase 4 to Phase 1**, because §0.1 says it is the only work
that changes what the site is. Effort excludes writing.

### Phase 0 — Foundations. Invisible to the user. ~0.5–1 day

- One `SECTIONS` list in `main.js`: `{ id, label, nodeId, caption, complication }`.
  `CAPTIONS`, the five `bind()` calls and the bar all read from it. `ZOOM_ORIGINS`
  and the `.hint-mark` coordinates stay as measured constants, filed next to it.
- Hit targets become focusable buttons with `aria-label`s, and `:focus-visible`
  gets the same light treatment hover already has. **This closes the site's one
  outright correctness bug** and it is cheap now and expensive later.
- Fix the stuck-hover case while you are in `bind()`.
- Escape also clears `state.flipped`.
- **Deletions — two of three already landed.** The review-request form is gone
  (`52f5b14`) and the `sessionStorage` intro-skip is gone (`175f476`). Note the
  intro-skip removal took its CSS delay-collapse rules with it, so §7.2's plan to
  repoint them at deep links now means re-adding those rules rather than
  retargeting surviving ones — a small extra step, not a blocker. Still to do:
  the three.js lab and the retired SVG movement generators.
- Update `HANDOFF.md:33` to record why three.js is out.

*Risk: low. All mechanical. The deletions are the bulk of the diff and they only
remove.*

### Phase 1 — Real content. ~0.5 day of code, plus the writing

**The phase that converts a demo into a website.** Nineteen fabricated items go.

- Panel copy moves to `src/content.js`, rendered by a small template function. The
  watch markup is not touched. New content in a new file = lowest merge risk while
  other agents work on the watch.
- Real About copy. A real portrait. Real Experience entries. A downloadable CV.
- **Four honest sections beat seven padded ones.** Ship what is real; leave the
  rest out of the list entirely rather than filling it.

*Risk: this is the phase most likely to stall, and it cannot be worked around. It
is also the only one that matters to a visitor.*

### Phase 2 — The index bar. ~1 day

- `#hintBar` becomes the live index, painted from `SECTIONS` and `state.active`.
- Stays visible when a panel is open (`z-index: 50`).

## 11. Still open

Eight questions became three, and none of them block Phase 0 or Phase 1.

1. **Top bar or bottom index bar?** §4 recommends the bottom bar and gives the
   measured reason (the vertical axis is already spent; the left margin belongs to
   the watch when a panel is open). §4.7 states the terms that would make a top bar
   work if you want conventionality more than the 7% of watch diameter it costs.
   **This is the one place where a different answer from you changes the plan's
   shape**, so it is worth a decision before Phase 2.

2. **Does anything need its own preview card?** §7.1's question: do you expect to
   paste a link to one specific project or essay and want it to preview with *that
   piece's* title and picture? No → hash links are the entire answer and Phase 7
   never happens. Yes → Phase 7 exists, after the writing does.

3. **The tilt clamp angle.** ±10° to start. Genuinely your eye, not a decision
   anyone else can make: it is the angle at which the baked sunburst dial stops
   reading as brushed metal. One CSS variable; judge it on a screenshot at maximum
   tilt.

Two things flagged rather than asked, because they are consequences you should see
coming rather than choices:

- **The 4.11s intro now plays on every visit** (§7.3). If it grates, the fix is an
  interruptible intro, not a return to memory.
- **The interaction index now scribes on every visit too**, since `state.touched`
  never persists. Correct given the no-memory decision, and slightly louder than
  before.

## 12. If you read only one page

- **The lorem is the whole game.** Nineteen invented items across five panels. A
  beautiful shell around dummy text reads as a demo, not as a person's site, and
  nothing else in this plan can compensate. It moves to Phase 1.
- **The panels, not the navigation, are the slop risk.** The card is a generic
  dark-mode component sitting inside a watch that is measured to two decimal
  places. Open About at 900×760 and you will see it immediately.
- **Neither a top bar nor a left rail fits.** The vertical axis is already spent
  (58px free at 1440×900) and the left margin belongs to the watch whenever a
  panel is open (the case reaches x=30). The bottom bar already exists as
  `#hintBar`; promote it. One design, every viewport, zero layout cost.
- **Contact goes in the empty bottom lens of the caseback band**, above the
  hallmark, as four engraved words that take the light on hover, with the actual
  address read out in the caption line. Nothing goes inside the sapphire.
- **Deletions are progress.** The review form, the dead three.js lab, the session
  memory. Three things gone in Phase 0, and the site is better for each.
