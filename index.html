GreenChain

HACKPRINCETON · APR 2026

Ranks real manufacturers on emissions, transport CO₂, grid carbon, and climate risk. An agent swarm finds the candidates, XGBoost models score them, a Three.js globe draws them. It won three tracks and was a grand-prize finalist.
BUILTA FastAPI service under uvicorn. One Dedalus runner call per search hands Claude Sonnet five tools — Brave search, a page fetcher and three scorers — over at most fourteen steps; three XGBoost quantile regressors then rank what comes back, q10/q50/q90 across 55 features, at a Spearman of 0.843 on 3,977 held-out rows. Transport is GLEC factors per tonne-km against port distances, and the composite weights five dimensions, manufacturing heaviest at 0.40. Next.js 16 and React 19 in front.
Prophis

YHACK · MAR 2026

Simulates public-health interventions across US counties on a D3 choropleth, over County Health Rankings data. A second view projects one person's risk ten years out.
BUILTAn Express API in TypeScript, deployed as Vercel functions, over 3,159 county records. Each of the twenty interventions carries per-indicator effect sizes, put through a saturation curve on spend per head, a targeting multiplier and a square-root time scale, then priced in QALYs with a ±13% band. Peer counties come from a weighted Euclidean distance over six demographic axes, and the individual view walks five risk scores forward a year at a time. React 19, Vite and zustand in front.

MAY — JUL 2026

A proof of concept with no scoring behind it yet, presented to the health ministries of Indonesia and South Sudan for potential collaboration.

Research

Harvard Medical School

Undergraduate Researcher, Blavatnik Institute

Cambridge, MA · HybridJuly 2024 — Present

I work with Dr. Kee B. Park on passive, phone-based detection of early neurological impairment, and in practice that's training multimodal deep-learning classifiers on audio and image samples to pick up neuromotor and speech biomarkers.

  • Movement and speech start to go before anyone gets a diagnosis: tremor, bradykinesia, and postural instability on the motor side, dysarthria and falling verbal fluency in speech. Facial palsy shows up in the image. The conditions are Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and ischemic stroke.
  • A model that only works on the population it was trained on is not the point. The models have to hold up across countries and health systems, and in the long run they need to work as well in a low-resource setting as in a high one.
  • The argument for the approach, co-authored with Park: AANS Neurosurgeon, November 2024.

Johns Hopkins School of Medicine

Undergraduate Researcher, Laboratory of Computational Intensive Care Medicine

Baltimore, MD · On-siteNovember 2025 — Present

Under Dr. Robert David Stevens, I work on machine-learning models that stratify the risk of complications after an elective craniotomy. I develop them on MIMIC-IV and evaluate them on a Johns Hopkins PMAP cohort.

  • The models put patients in order of risk, and that's all they do. They don't diagnose anyone, and the ranking says nothing about which complication or when.
  • Somebody has to decide how closely a patient is watched once they leave the operating room, and that's the one decision the ranking is for.
  • Each model refits its own feature selection inside every training fold — a penalised logistic regression, XGBoost, CatBoost, and a stack of the three under a ridge meta-learner. The second cohort is for testing the models, never for training them. Nobody knows yet whether the ranking still means anything when the patients, the surgeons, and the recording habits change.
